## First-week plan

**Before launch:** open the live app in a private browser, check the three scenarios, inspect the repository/source link, review the current community rules and account eligibility, and tailor the wording in your own voice. Use your real account; do not invent user results, buy engagement or ask for traded stars.

**Day 0:** publish the short X demo and keep time available to answer replies. Make one clearly disclosed, relevant community post only if permitted. Link the working demo first and source second. A same-day reply to a relevant conversation can be useful, but do not paste the same reply everywhere.

**Days 1–2:** ask five willing developers to try their own category summary. Ask whether the first comparison worked, whether missing data was clear, and which format failed. A synthetic/redacted reproduction is enough; never request raw transcripts or credentials. Do not DM strangers en masse.

**Days 3–4:** fix the most frequent genuine failure; publish a concrete changelog and a new example with permission. Add tested aliases/formats instead of adding a dozen unrelated features. Reply to bug reports and explain limitations honestly.

**Day 7:** review GitHub traffic/referrers/clones alongside self-reported successful comparisons, issues, stars and forks. Traffic is a rolling aggregate and may include bots; clones are not unique active users. Zero visits suggests distribution; visits without successful comparisons suggests onboarding/product issues. These are diagnostic hypotheses, not causal proof.

## Success test

Initial target: five independently confirmed useful comparisons, two useful format reports and one improvement made from feedback. Stars/forks are secondary outcomes we also track. There is no evidence-based promise of a specific star count or GitHub Trending placement.

If you get 100 relevant visits and no one confirms a useful result, interview users and revise the value proposition before creating another repository. Avoid changing both the product and distribution every day: it makes the result hard to interpret.

## Tracking without app telemetry

Keep a private table with date, channel/post URL, visitors/referrers/clones reported by GitHub, voluntary useful-comparison reports, issues, stars and forks. Do not commit identifying visitor data or private analytics. The app itself makes no analytics requests. Social draft links do not contain tracking identifiers.

No paid ads, accounts, platform integrations or scheduled outreach are created by this repository. Posting still requires a selected account and actual platform access; a draft is not a published advertisement.
